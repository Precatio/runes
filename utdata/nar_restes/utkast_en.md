*UlfR hafiR a Ænglandi þry giald takit.*

Ulfr has taken three payments in England.

So says a stone at Yttergärde in Orkesta parish, Uppland (U 344). The first payment was paid by Tosti, the second by Þorketill and the third by Knútr. As early as 1909 Otto von Friesen showed that the stone speaks of events in England between 980 and 1018. Knútr is Cnut the Great, who in 1018 paid his Scandinavian warriors 82,500 pounds (von Friesen 1909, pp. 65 and 70). It is one of very few runestones that point to a year in the world that wrote history.

Yet the runic database places the stone in style group Pr3, usually dated to 1050–1080. Is that a mistake? No. It is the most important thing to know about how runestones are dated.

## How a runestone gets its age

Runestones carry no years. What most of them have is ornament: a rune band shaped like an animal, with head and tail, and sometimes a cross. The archaeologist Anne-Sofie Gräslund has ordered the ornament into style groups, from RAK, where the band has no animal head, to Pr5, where the animals are thin and tightly interlaced. Each group has been given approximate years: RAK about 980–1015, Fp and Pr1 1010–1050, Pr2 1020–1050, Pr3 1050–1080, Pr4 1070–1100 and Pr5 1100–1130.

Of the 2,321 Swedish Viking-age runestones in the database, 1,038 have a certain style group, 45 per cent. 438 have an uncertain one – a question mark or a range such as "Pr3–Pr4" – and 845 have none at all, usually because the stone is damaged, lost or has no animal ornament. Only 102 stones have their own time indication in the dating field, such as "late 10th century" or "about 1100". The rest are simply "V", Viking Age.

The scale is coarser than it looks. Taking the middle of each group gives six different years for the whole material: 997.5, 1030, 1035, 1065, 1085 and 1115. It is like dividing a century into six hours of the clock. And style and language have largely dated each other: the ornament has been ordered with the help of linguistic features, and the linguistic features with the help of the ornament. Points outside both are needed.

## The fixed points

There are two such groups, and both come from journeys.

**The Ingvarr stones.** About 25 Swedish runestones commemorate men who died with Ingvarr on his expedition east. The Icelandic saga about Ingvarr says that he died in 1041. Alf Thulin wrote in 1985:

> The saga says that Ingvarr died in 1041, a statement which, despite its unreliability, has become the firmest point in the chronology of the Uppland runestones.

(Thulin 1985, p. 201; our translation.) The year is disputed. Annett Krakow proposed about 1020 in 2021; Mats G. Larsson held to about 1040. But what makes the stones valuable is something else: the men died on the journey, so the stones were probably raised within a few years. The event and the stone lie close together in time.

The ornament of the Ingvarr stones agrees fairly well. Of the 25, 15 belong to style group Fp, 2 to Pr2 and one to RAK – Skarði's stone at Lundby (Sö 131). Seven have no certain style group.

**The England stones.** 22 Swedish runestones name England. Their events – the payments and Cnut's army – belong to the decades before and after the year 1000, up to Cnut the Great's death in 1035. But the stones' style groups spread out: 7 RAK, 4 Fp, 3 Pr2, 4 Pr3 and 4 without a certain group. Four of them thus belong to a time after 1050.

Mats G. Larsson gave the explanation: the England stones were often raised for men who came home safely and got their stone only when they died at home (Larsson 2021, p. 244, after Larsson 1990). Ulfr at Yttergärde took three payments as a young man. The stone was raised when he was dead, perhaps a generation later. **An event on a runestone dates the man's life. The style group tries to date the stone.** The two need not agree, and when they do not, nothing is wrong.

That makes the Ingvarr stones the better fixed point – and it makes them almost the only one.

## What happens to a pattern?

Anyone who wants to know how something changed during the 11th century must place the stones on a timeline. Then only those with a certain style group count – fewer than half.

Take the prayer, *Guð hialpi*, "God help his soul". Among stones in the early style groups (RAK, Fp, Pr1 and Pr2) it appears on 18 per cent. Among the late ones (Pr3–Pr5) on 11 per cent. It looks as if the prayer became less common.

But 1,283 stones have no certain style group, and 86 of them carry the prayer. Where do they belong?

- If they were all early, the prayer would appear on 10 per cent of the early stones and 11 per cent of the late. The pattern reverses.
- If they were all late, it would be 18 against 8 per cent. The pattern becomes stronger.

Neither extreme is true. The undated stones are both early and late. But the worked example shows how much rests on them: the direction of a pattern through the 11th century can depend on stones we cannot place. Anyone who reads that "the runestones show that something increased" should ask how large a share of the stones was in the count – and whether the missing ones may have been different.

They may. The undated stones are more often damaged and more often hard to interpret, and they are unevenly spread across the country. In Västergötland 63 per cent of the runestones have a certain style group, in Södermanland 52 and in Uppland 48 – but on Öland 14, on Gotland 21 and in Småland 26 per cent. And more than half of all stones with a style group stand in Uppland, 567 of 1,038. A time curve for "Sweden" is in practice largely a curve for Uppland.

## What we do about it

Vitki never compares periods without also taking the region into account, and we test every pattern in two halves of the country separately. We have also tried to give the undated stones an age from their language and text, under locked plans. The attempts did not meet our own requirements, so they are not used. The coarse scale of the style groups cannot be refined with the data available today.

What can change the picture is new fixed points: stones found in a dated grave or foundation, dendro-dated timber in the same layer, coins. Every such stone is worth more for dating than a hundred new style assessments.

## What we do not know

- **The years of the style groups are approximate.** They overlap, and they are partly anchored in the Ingvarr expedition itself.
- **The Ingvarr expedition is itself disputed,** with proposals from about 1020 to 1041.
- **The prayer is an example, not a result.** We have not tested whether the prayer really declined; the figures only show how far the undated stones can move a pattern.
- **The editors' own time indications** (102 stones) often rest on language and style too.

:::box The main points in brief
- Fewer than half of the Swedish runestones have a certain date, and almost all dates rest on the ornament. The whole scale has six different years.
- There are few fixed points outside the style. The Ingvarr stones are the best, because the men died on the journey. The England stones date the men's lives, not the stones – they were often raised much later.
- When dating is uncertain, the direction of a pattern through the 11th century can depend on the stones that cannot be placed. The prayer looks as if it became rarer – or commoner – depending on where the undated stones belong.
- New fixed points, such as stones in dated contexts, are what can improve the dating.
:::
